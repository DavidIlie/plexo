## Design Context

### Users

Plexo serves both private household dashboards and public-facing, read-only views of a personal Plex library. Owners use it to understand their library, progress, and viewing patterns; household members and visitors use it to browse the collection and recent activity. Deployments may contain several Plex users, so personal viewing data must never be assumed to be safe to disclose.

### Brand Personality

Quiet, cinematic, and precise. Plexo should feel like a considered companion to a media library: compact and information-rich without becoming clinical, playful without becoming ornamental, and trustworthy around personal data.

### Aesthetic Direction

Preserve the existing warm amber identity, subtly tinted neutral surfaces, restrained dark-first presentation, and compact application typography. Identity cues should feel native to a streaming activity feed, not like social-network chrome. Both light and dark themes remain first-class.

### Design Principles

- Privacy is a deployment choice: default to hiding viewer identities, then let owners deliberately reveal avatars, names, or both.
- Reveal multi-user context only where it disambiguates data; do not repeat identities on single-user views.
- Keep controls quiet and progressive, with richer filtering on dedicated pages rather than crowding the dashboard.
- Treat avatars as supporting metadata, with accessible text alternatives and resilient fallbacks.
- Maintain strong mobile usability, keyboard access, reduced-motion support, and readable contrast.
